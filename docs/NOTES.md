# Notes

Choices I made while building this, and the reasons for them.

## Data

- **LHX backlog gaps.** LHX's 10-Qs for the first three quarters of 2020 and 2021 never tag `RevenueRemainingPerformanceObligation` at the quarter's own end. The 2020Q1 filing tags none, and the rest tag only the prior fiscal year end (2020-01-03 or 2021-01-01). Quarterly tagging starts with the 10-Q for 2022Q1 (accession 0000202058-22-000029). I kept LHX as preregistered. The backlog model skips any forecast that needs one of those six quarters, so it scores fewer LHX forecasts than the two baselines.
- **HII's backlog jump.** HII's tagged backlog goes from $23.0B at 2018Q4 to $40.6B at 2019Q1. I found no filing sentence that explains it, so the README describes it and stops there.
- **Raw files stay out of git.** `make data` fetches a companyfacts file only when it is missing, and `data/raw/MANIFEST.json` records each file's hash and size.

## Model

- **Bridge residual.** I compute the residual as the revenue change less the sum of the three effects, which follows the preregistered wording. Subtracting the effects one at a time moves the float result by about a millionth of a dollar. Either way it is rounding noise.
- **Pooled comparisons.** The baselines score more forecasts than the backlog model because of the LHX gaps. `report.py` also ranks the three models on the forecasts they share and prints whether the ranking holds. It does.

## Workbook

- Every sheet except `About` freezes panes at `B2`. `About` is a single column of text.
- openpyxl stamps the save time into the file, so I pin the document dates and zip entry times to the as-of date. A rebuild writes identical bytes.

## Dashboard

- **Layout.** The title sits at top left, and the global filters (company, horizon, period) and two icon buttons (shortcuts, theme) at top right, on one sticky row from 1280 px. Under them is the overview, on the same two columns as the charts: the finding beside the pooled KPIs, then the scorecard that breaks those down by company beside the selected company's KPIs. Then come the selected company's panels, each with its ticker in the heading: backlog and revenue beside the forecasts, the revenue bridge at full width, and the what-if. Last are the granular table and the method. Below 1200 px everything goes to one column, and below 900 px the header stops being sticky. On a phone the two icon buttons sit beside the title and every filter stays in view.
- **The finding.** The plain answer to the title's question is the largest line on the page, with the full-sample sentence that carries the figures under it, then what the finding covers, then the source line (basis, source, period, date). The answer leads because a reader arriving from a link should have it in the first three seconds.
- **Type and colour.** One face, IBM Plex Sans, self-hosted as a variable woff2 at 400, 500 and 600, with a local fallback sized to its metrics so nothing moves when it swaps in. Its digits are all one width, so tables and chart ticks line up without a monospaced face. Running text is 14 px and nothing is read below 12 px. The page is warm paper with near-black ink and one ink blue; sections open on an ink rule rather than in boxes. Every size, weight, colour, space and duration is a token in `site/style.css`.
- **KPIs.** Three pooled KPIs (miss, win rate, bias) and four for the selected company. The forecast counts are a column of the scorecard, not a card. Each cell's head, caption, value and variance sit on row tracks shared across its strip (CSS subgrid), so values line up however the labels wrap. Beside the scorecard, and on phones, the cells become rows with the figure at the right. The pooled miss and win rate repeat the scorecard's All five row by construction: both come from one `summarize()` result.
- **Scorecard.** A variance table: a row for all five companies pooled and one for each, the three models' average miss, then the backlog model's difference from the baseline and its win rate, each with a bar on one scale for its column (the widest difference in view, and ±50 points around a coin flip). The forecasts-scored column shows the backlog model's count of the baseline's, since the baselines also score origins without tagged backlog. Δ is the difference of the averages as published; the note under the table gives it again on the origins both models score (`sharedSummary()` in `model.js`).
- **Scales.** The backlog chart starts at zero for every company, so the gap between backlog and trailing revenue reads at its true size. The bridge needs a cut axis for its steps to show, so its two total bars, which measure from zero, carry break marks, and the caption says the axis starts above zero.
- **One data model.** `site/js/model.js` flattens `data.json` once into quarter rows, forecast rows, bridge rows and the what-if grid. The KPIs, every chart and the table read only those rows, so a chart and the table under it cannot disagree. `summarize()` computes the error statistics for any filter, and the KPIs, the scorecard and the table footer all call it. `sharedSummary()` runs it on the origins every model scores.
- **Reconciliation.** `site/js/reconcile.js` recomputes, in whole cents, every trailing four-quarter figure, every bridge year's revenue, each bridge's steps, and every forecast's actual, from the quarterly rows. It also recomputes the published average misses and win rates from the forecast rows, the scorecard's like-for-like miss, each bridge's starting backlogs and conversion rates, and the what-if's starting conversion rate. The footer of the table shows the result for the companies and period in view. `tests/test_site_data.py` asserts the same identities on `data.json` in CI.
- **Bridge rounding.** Rounding the three bridge effects to the cent leaves a one-cent gap in three company-years (HII 2024, LHX 2023, LMT 2021). The overlap is shown as the balancing figure, so the steps add to the change exactly. The table beside the chart rounds each figure once, to $0.1M, with the overlap again as the balancing figure, so the printed column foots too. Beside it, the driver table gives each year's starting backlog and annual conversion rate, the inputs the three effects are worked from. Its conversion rates have two decimals, so the effects can be followed from them.
- **Rounding.** A change is worked from unrounded figures, so it can differ by one in its last place from the difference of the printed figures. A change is coloured as it prints, so one that rounds to zero is never red or green. Coverage shows two decimals in its value, comparison and change, so a small change never sits between two figures that print alike. The bridge caption gives conversion to one decimal. In whole percents, three company-years (HII 2024, RTX 2022, RTX 2025) read as unchanged beside a conversion effect the chart shows.
- **Red and green.** Red and green appear only on a variance: an error against the baseline, the win rate against 50%, growth year on year, coverage against a year earlier, book-to-bill against 1.0×, and bridge steps. Each carries ▲ or ▼ and a sign as well. Series colours are ink blue, ink and two warm greys, never red or green. Bias, the what-if change and the what-if grid are shown without colour, since neither direction is good or bad in itself.
- **Units and basis.** Every figure names its unit and scale (US$ billions in charts and KPIs, US$ millions in the table) and its basis: GAAP as first reported, derived from GAAP, or model. Tooltips give each reported figure in both $B and $M, so the $M figure can be found in the table.
- **Audit trail.** `data.json` also carries each quarter's period end, the accession numbers of the filings its backlog and revenue came from, and whether Q4 revenue is the full year less nine months. The table links each accession to its EDGAR folder. These keys are additive; the existing keys are unchanged.
- **States.** Every panel starts as `data-state="loading"` in the HTML, with a skeleton of its final size in the default view (Lockheed Martin, next four quarters, every year). Each slot the data fills, the header's included, holds a hidden stand-in: the text or table that will replace it, with a # for each digit. In Plex a # is wider than a digit, so a small face that covers # alone scales it to a digit's width; a stand-in then wraps as the real text will at any width, and the HTML holds no figures. Notes carry their final wording. The KPI strips draw skeleton bars over a stand-in of each card, and the tables and the scenario grid show as blocks, as the charts do. While loading, the loading messages are for screen readers only and the screen shows only skeletons; a failure shows the page-level message with Try again, and each panel says quietly what it will show. With `data.json` held back, no panel moves more than 0.1 px when it arrives at 1440, 1280 or 390 px wide. Another company or view from the URL, or a width where a line only just wraps, can still move the panels below it by a line or two; the header keeps one row for every company's name at 1280 and 1440 px. A failed download, a bad response or a throw while starting shows a page-level error with Try again. Each panel draws inside its own guard: a throw marks only that panel as failed. A filter that leaves a panel with nothing to show gives it an empty state that says why, with Show all years when the period was narrowed. Under an empty or failed panel's message, the old content keeps its space but is hidden, so it leaves the tab order and the accessibility tree. The table says when no rows match and offers Clear filters.
- **Table performance.** Rows are flattened once with a lowercase search key. A filter and sort result is memoised on its inputs, the search waits for 150 ms of quiet, and rows are written 200 at a time as one HTML string. Export CSV writes the filtered rows at full precision in US dollars.
- **What-if.** It always starts from the latest quarter, so the period filter does not apply to it, and the panel names the quarters it covers. The readout states its inputs: today's backlog and the four-quarter conversion rate implied by the published no-change scenario, each with its change. It states them as inputs rather than as a product, since rounded inputs need not multiply back to the rounded total. This four-quarter rate is not the bridge's annual conversion rate, and each has its own name. The readout shows trailing four-quarter revenue and the backtest bias next to the scenario, since the no-change scenario can sit well above recent revenue. The fan has a key and span labels, which separate the reported (GAAP) history from the model scenarios. Its lines join to each scenario's total and are not quarter-by-quarter paths.
- **Replay.** The forecast chart can play back in the order the forecasts were made. Under reduced motion the Play button is hidden and the scrubber still steps through.
- **Linked crosshairs.** The book-to-bill strip shares the backlog chart's quarters and y-axis width. Pointing at any of the backlog, book-to-bill and forecast charts draws a dashed crosshair at the same quarter in the other two.
- **No decoration.** The backdrop sparkline, the how-to strip, the staggered intro and the count-up numbers are gone. Chart updates animate for 250 ms, and not at all under reduced motion.
- The theme button saves its choice in `localStorage` and applies it before the first paint. Without a saved choice, the page follows the system setting.
- The front end is native ES modules under `site/js/`, with no build step. Chart.js is vendored and loaded first as a classic script.
- GitHub Pages serves the site at `/Defense-Backlog-forecast/`, matching the repository name. The lowercase path returns 404.

## Accessibility

- I checked the dashboard against WCAG 2.2 AA. `tests/test_site_a11y.py` runs axe-core (vendored in `tests/vendor`, so it needs no network) in headless Chromium. It runs in light, dark, forced colours, more contrast and at 390 px, with a definition and the reconciliation list open, and again with empty panels. The same file checks the skip link, the company filter's arrow keys, the shortcut switch, reading a chart from the keyboard, scenario announcements, that focus never lands under the sticky header, reconciliation for each company, the empty and error states, and the table's search, sort and export. Without Playwright installed the file is skipped. `make a11y` runs it on its own.
- The company, horizon and bridge-year controls are radio groups or pressed buttons: one tab stop, with the arrow keys, Home and End moving through them.
- `1`–`5`, `R` and `?` are single-key shortcuts, which speech input can set off by accident. "Shortcuts" in the header lists them and has a switch that turns them off. The choice is saved in `localStorage` (`dbf-keys`). The arrow keys and Escape keep working either way.
- Each chart takes focus. The arrow keys, Home and End step through its points and show the same tooltip and linked crosshairs a pointer would. A screen reader hears the tooltip's rows. Escape or leaving the chart clears it.
- One polite live region speaks what a change did once it settles: the filters after a change, and the scenario after a slider or grid change. Spoken text says "billion", "million" and "percentage points" where the page shows "B", "M" and "pp".
- The sticky header's height changes as the filters wrap. A ResizeObserver keeps `scroll-padding-top` at its measured height, and anything that takes focus under it is scrolled clear.
- Every variance carries ▲ or ▼ and a sign, and the KPI cards and the scorecard add "favourable" or "unfavourable" for screen readers, so no meaning rests on colour. The scorecard's variance bars are hidden from screen readers; the figure beside each carries its value.
- An empty or failed panel hides what its message covers, so focus never lands on a chart or button under the message (WCAG 2.4.11). The a11y tests tab through an empty period to check.
- The overview is one section with a visually hidden "Summary" heading, so its four blocks are h3 under it and the heading order holds.
- Under forced colours (Windows High Contrast), selection uses the system highlight and the line swatches keep their own colours. The scorecard's bars draw in the text colour, and its selected row keeps a rule at its left edge.
- The type scale is in rem, so a larger default text size carries through. When the what-if grid is narrow, its cells drop the dollar sign; their accessible names keep it.
- Links in the table are at least 24 px tall (WCAG 2.5.8). On a touch screen every control is at least 44 px, and each info button's target reaches 44 px around its small mark.
- Each icon button's name shows as a tooltip on hover and keyboard focus, and is its accessible name either way.
